Emmanuel Amissah Sampson is a Master of Urban Spatial Analytics candidate in the Department of City and Regional Planning at the University of Pennsylvania's Stuart Weitzman School of Design, where he is a Stuart Weitzman Scholar. His research spans spatial, environmental and development economics. He studies how the location choices of firms and public facilities create spillovers across urban networks, how those choices shape travel, emissions and exposure to urban heat, and how public investment and digital technology shape markets and access to land and services in African cities.

Before Penn, Emmanuel spent more than a decade bringing geospatial technology to governments, utilities, mines and donor-funded programmes across Africa: first at Trimble (2015–2023), where he won $85M in competitive bids and secured site permits for national GNSS reference station networks used in land administration, and then as Geospatial Regional Manager for East and Central Africa at Leica Geosystems (Hexagon) (2025–2026). Earlier, he co-led value-chain research for the Cocoa Life programme in Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire with CARE International.

He holds an MSc in Development Management from the London School of Economics, an MBA with Distinction from Lancaster University, and a BSc in Business Administration with First Class Honours from KNUST, Ghana. He is the author of seven journal articles on AI, digital transformation and financial inclusion, and was a Perplexity AI Business Fellow in 2025. He grew up in Obuasi, Ghana, and mentors students from Ghana seeking graduate study in the United States and the United Kingdom.

Research

Location, environment and development

My research sits at the meeting point of spatial, environmental and development economics. Its organising question is how the location decisions of firms, public facilities and infrastructure ripple outward: through demand and competition at neighbouring sites, through the travel, emissions and heat exposure of the households around them, and through access to markets, land and services in fast-growing African cities. I combine applied microeconomic reasoning with spatial econometrics, causal inference from natural experiments, machine learning and remote sensing.

Area 1

Spatial economics of location choice

How the entry and exit of stores, clinics, restaurants and banks create spillovers across a network of locations, and what these spillovers mean for competition, prices and consumer access.

  • Store incursion and trade areas. How a new outlet draws demand from the catchments of existing ones. Current
  • Pharmacy closures and access to care. The 2023–2025 Rite Aid closures as a natural experiment in travel burden and health access. Planned

Area 2

Environmental and resource economics

The environmental costs and benefits that follow from where activity locates: travel and emissions, urban heat, green space and the financing of climate-resilient infrastructure.

  • Commercial location, travel and emissions. How store entry and exit change household travel and the resulting emissions, compared across US and African cities. Planned
  • Urban heat in Philadelphia. Machine-learning prediction of surface heat from land cover and built form, to target cooling investment where it does most good. Current
  • Housing stress and greenness. Evictions and code violations set against vegetation (NDVI) across Philadelphia neighbourhoods. Current
  • Low-carbon mobility. A siting model for Indego bike-share stations that weighs demand, transit ridership and bike-lane coverage. Current

Area 3

Development economics and public policy

How public investment, digital technology and institutions shape markets and livelihoods in Africa, drawing on more than a decade of fieldwork with governments and donor-funded programmes.

  • Land administration and property rights. How national GNSS reference networks lower the cost of surveying and land titling.
  • Digital transformation and trade. Digital sales and cross-border trade in Sub-Saharan Africa, and the role of technology in the Sustainable Development Goals.
  • Agricultural value chains. Value-chain research for the Cocoa Life programme in Ghana and Côte d'Ivoire.
  • Infrastructure finance. Public-private partnership design and donor finance for geospatial infrastructure.
