Biography

Emmanuel Amissah Sampson is a Master of Urban Spatial Analytics candidate in the Department of City and Regional Planning at the University of Pennsylvania’s Stuart Weitzman School of Design, where he is a Stuart Weitzman Scholar. He studies how the location choices of firms and public facilities create spillovers across urban networks, how those choices shape access to everyday services and health care, and how digital technology shapes markets and access to land and services in African cities.

Before Penn, he spent more than a decade bringing geospatial technology to governments, utilities, mines and donor-funded programmes across Africa: first at Trimble (2015–2023), where he won $85M in competitive bids and secured site permits for national GNSS reference station networks used in land administration, and then as Geospatial Regional Manager for East and Central Africa at Leica Geosystems (Hexagon) (2025–2026). Earlier, he co-led value-chain research for the Cocoa Life programme in Ghana and Côte d’Ivoire with CARE International.

He holds an MSc in Development Management from the London School of Economics, an MBA with Distinction from Lancaster University, and a BSc in Business Administration with First Class Honours from KNUST, Ghana. He is the author of seven journal articles on AI, digital transformation and financial inclusion, and was a Perplexity AI Business Fellow in 2025. He grew up in Obuasi, Ghana, and mentors students from Ghana seeking graduate study in the United States and the United Kingdom.

Research

My research sits at the meeting point of spatial and urban economics, health policy and digital transformation. Its organising question is how the location decisions of firms, public facilities and infrastructure ripple outward: through demand and competition at neighbouring sites, through households’ access to everyday services and health care, and through the digital systems that increasingly shape where services go and who can reach them, in American cities and in fast-growing African cities.

Health access and policy

Pharmacy Closures and Access in Philadelphia, 2023–2025

Emmanuel A. Sampson · Research report, University of Pennsylvania, November 2026

Using a 41-location evidence ledger built from court filings, company transfer tables and insurer directories, I route walks and drives from 62 census-tract origins to each former Rite Aid and to its designated recipient on a fixed 2025 street network. The population-weighted modeled walk rises from 9.0 to 16.0 minutes, with larger increases in higher-poverty tracts. The report sets out a spatial difference-in-differences design for measuring actual travel and emissions.

Commercial location and access

Grocery Closures and Neighborhood Access in Chicago, 2021–2025

Emmanuel A. Sampson · Research report, University of Pennsylvania, November 2026

Six store closures and a replacement opening, verified against USDA SNAP retailer records and food inspections, with routed access compared from the same tract origins before and after each event. Modeled walks around the Kenwood Walmart rise from 11.5 to 16.1 minutes, while a Save A Lot opening in Englewood restores the proximity lost when Whole Foods closed.

Area 1

Commercial location and access

How the entry and exit of stores, clinics, restaurants and banks create spillovers across a network of locations, and what these spillovers mean for competition, prices and consumer access.

  • Store incursion and trade areas. How a new outlet draws demand from the catchments of existing ones.
  • Closures, travel and access. How store entry and exit change how far households travel for everyday goods and services, compared across U.S. and African cities. Planned

Area 2

Health access and policy

Where health services locate, what happens to patients when they leave, and how policy can protect access for the people with the fewest alternatives.

  • Health programme delivery. Procurement and logistics for a five-year, $150 million Global Fund malaria-control programme in Obuasi, Ghana.

Area 3

Digital transformation

How digital technology, data and AI reshape markets, public services and access to opportunity, drawing on more than a decade of work with governments and donor-funded programmes in Africa.

  • Digital transformation and trade. Digital sales and cross-border trade in Sub-Saharan Africa, and the role of technology in the Sustainable Development Goals.
  • AI and financial inclusion. How AI-enabled financial services affect access for underserved populations, and how governance shapes who benefits.
  • Land administration and property rights. How national GNSS reference networks lower the cost of surveying and land titling.

Area 4

Spatial analytics

Spatial econometrics, machine learning and remote sensing applied to urban data, to show where needs are greatest and where investment does most good.

  • Tree canopy from space. Estimating Philadelphia’s tree canopy from Sentinel-2 imagery, comparing machine-learning and deep-learning models. Current
  • Housing stress and greenness. Evictions and code violations set against vegetation (NDVI) across Philadelphia neighbourhoods. Current
  • Bike-share siting. A siting model for Indego stations that weighs demand, transit ridership and bike-lane coverage. Current

Methods. Applied microeconometrics and causal inference; spatial econometrics and spatial interdependence; trade-area and catchment analysis; machine learning for prediction; remote sensing; GNSS and survey data. Tools: R, Python (GeoPandas), SQL, ArcGIS Pro, QGIS and GeoDa.
